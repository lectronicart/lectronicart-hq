## 1. The Thesis

LectronicArt exists to help creative people — artists, video makers, content creators, and one-person digital builders — build their own AI-powered creative machine: the systems, workflows, and automations that let one person produce like a studio.

The founding insight is that **the curriculum is the product**. You are not building a roadmap and then, separately, a business. The roadmap *is* the business in larval form. Every day you spend learning produces an artifact — a journal entry, a working pipeline, a template, a tutorial, a mistake worth narrating — and those artifacts, organized, become the classroom. The struggle, documented honestly, becomes the marketing. The transformation, completed publicly, becomes the proof.

This is why the answer to your question — "so what I use to teach myself ends up being the product I sell?" — is yes, but the deeper version is this: **what you sell is the transformation you personally underwent, made repeatable.** In June 2026 you couldn't read a 50-line JavaScript file. By February 2027 you'll have shipped a product, built agents, automated art and video pipelines, and launched a community. Nobody can fact-check that journey better than your own public archive — and nobody can teach it more credibly than the person who just walked it. Gurus teach from theory. You'll teach from receipts.

And you're right that it's bigger than the roadmap. The roadmap is the *spine*. The body is everything you listed — and Section 3 gives each of those things a formal place in the system so none of them stays a vague ambition.

## 2. The Flywheel

Everything in LectronicArt runs on one loop:

**BUILD → DOCUMENT → TEACH → SELL → fund the next BUILD.**

You build a real system for yourself (a content engine, an art workflow, an automation). You document it while it's still fresh and hard — the confusion is the curriculum. You teach it publicly in plain language, which grows the audience. A fraction of the audience buys the packaged version (template, kit, community membership), which funds and motivates the next build. Each rotation makes every part stronger: better builds make better lessons, better lessons grow a better audience, a better audience funds better builds.

Two rules keep the flywheel honest:

**Rule one: never teach what you haven't built.** This is the teaching version of the vibe-coding trap. The AI education space is flooded with people repackaging other people's tutorials. Your moat is that every lesson in the classroom traces back to a dated commit, a journal entry, a thing that actually ran on your machine. The day you start teaching ahead of your own building, the brand becomes hollow.

**Rule two: the audience watches the middle, not just the end.** Building in public starts at Day 1, not launch week. People don't follow polished experts as loyally as they follow someone slightly ahead of them on the same path. Your weekly post from Week 3 — "I spent four hours stuck on a voting algorithm and here's the mental model that saved me" — recruits future members more effectively than any ad ever will.

## 3. The Ten Pillars

These are the things you named. Each one is a system you will build for yourself first (the calendar schedules when), then package for the community. Together they form the full LectronicArt catalog.

### Pillar 1 — Prompt Systems
**What it is:** Versioned, tested, reusable prompts and prompt chains — treated like code, not like magic words. Includes a personal prompt library structure, parameterized templates for creator tasks, and simple evals that prove a prompt works consistently.
**You build it:** Week 26.
**It becomes:** Your first digital product (Prompt System v1, given away free as market research, later expanded into the paid library). Also Module 2 of the classroom — and the foundation every other pillar sits on, because every workflow downstream is driven by prompts.

### Pillar 2 — AI Art Workflows
**What it is:** Repeatable image-generation pipelines — ComfyUI graphs, style-consistency systems, brand-locked visual engines, batch generation scripts. The difference between "I made a cool image once" and "I can produce 50 on-brand visuals in an hour."
**You build it:** Week 30 (with the API/scripting skills from Weeks 25–27 making it automatable).
**It becomes:** Downloadable workflow templates with plain-language guides, plus the visual identity engine for the LectronicArt brand itself. The artists in your community get their first taste of "automation" here, because it's the pillar closest to their existing craft.

### Pillar 3 — AI Video Workflows
**What it is:** The script → voiceover → captions → b-roll → finished-video pipeline. ffmpeg as the backbone, generation APIs for the creative layers, transcription for captions. One prompt in, publishable short video out.
**You build it:** Week 31.
**It becomes:** The flagship demo of the whole brand — a video made by the pipeline, about the pipeline, is the single most persuasive piece of content LectronicArt will produce. Packaged as both a code template and an n8n version for non-coders.

### Pillar 4 — Content Engines
**What it is:** The repurposing machine: one pillar piece of content goes in, and threads, short posts, captions, hooks, newsletter sections, and clips come out — with an agent critic pass that keeps everything in your voice.
**You build it:** Weeks 29 and 32.
**It becomes:** The system that runs LectronicArt's own marketing (eat your own cooking, publicly) and one of the highest-demand teachable systems, because every creator feels the repurposing pain weekly.

### Pillar 5 — Automation Templates
**What it is:** The productized form of everything above: cleaned-up, documented, installable versions of your real workflows. Consistent naming, consistent READMEs, demo GIFs, install scripts. A product line, not a junk drawer.
**You build it:** Week 33 (auditing eight months of builds and productizing the top five).
**It becomes:** The template vault — the core asset of the paid tier. Members don't just learn how you built something; they install it and modify it.

### Pillar 6 — Creator Operating Systems
**What it is:** The connective tissue: the Obsidian/Notion workspace that holds projects, the prompt library, the asset pipeline, the content calendar, and metrics. The "OS" a one-person creative business actually runs on. You've been prototyping this since 2025 with your agent memory vaults — this formalizes it.
**You build it:** Week 33.
**It becomes:** The LectronicArt Starter Kit's centerpiece — the first thing a new member installs, and the container every other pillar's outputs live inside.

### Pillar 7 — Digital Product Creation
**What it is:** The meta-skill: turning a working system into something a stranger can buy, install, and succeed with. Packaging, documentation, pricing, delivery, the free-to-paid ladder.
**You build it:** Continuously from Week 26 (first free product) through Week 34 (the full kit), with Phase 3 (Weeks 13–24) having already taught you product thinking the hard way.
**It becomes:** A classroom module of its own — because your members don't just want to use AI creatively; many want to sell what they make, and you'll have done it in front of them.

### Pillar 8 — Brand-Building Systems
**What it is:** The LectronicArt identity made systematic: visual style (powered by your own art workflow — Pillar 2 generating Pillar 8 is the kind of loop the brand is *about*), voice guidelines (encoded into your content engine's critic agent), positioning ("real builder, real process, plain language"), and the build-in-public cadence.
**You build it:** Threaded through everything — the weekly public post starts Week 1; the brand workflow lands Week 30; the story crystallizes in the Week 29 thesis document.
**It becomes:** A teachable system ("brand yourself with your own machine") and the reason your launch in Week 36 lands on warm ground instead of cold.

### Pillar 9 — Simple Business Experiments
**What it is:** The discipline of small bets: define a hypothesis, set a number, set a deadline, run it, learn either way. Phase 3's "first 10 users" is the prototype; the Week 35 founding-cohort offer (10 members in 7 days) is the second; everything after is a sequence of these.
**You build it:** Weeks 13–24 teach it; Weeks 34–36 formalize it; Q2 2027 onward, it's the operating rhythm.
**It becomes:** The community's monthly heartbeat — members run their own small experiments (first sale, first 100 followers, first template shipped) with the group as witness. Which leads directly to:

### Pillar 10 — Community Accountability
**What it is:** The actual product of the Skool community. The content is the excuse; the accountability is what people pay for and stay for. Weekly build challenges, ship-it Friday threads, a monthly live workshop where you build (and break, and fix) things live, and an onboarding path that gets every new member a real win within 48 hours.

## 4. The 252-Day Arc

The calendar executes this thesis in four phases. The operating principle shifts at each boundary:

**Phase 1 — Fundamentals (Weeks 1–6, Jun 15–Jul 26).** *AI explains, you write.* CS50x lectures 0–5 and Odin Foundations, zero AI-generated code. This is the moat under everything: you will be one of very few creative-automation teachers who can actually read the code their machine runs on.

**Phase 2 — Real Projects (Weeks 7–12, Jul 27–Sep 6).** *You design, AI drafts, you review.* Full Stack Open parts 0–3, Plan Mode as a habit, the explain-then-rebuild loop, three shipped apps.

**Phase 3 — Ship Your Product (Weeks 13–24, Sep 7–Nov 29).** *You orchestrate, AI executes.* Problem discovery → MVP → real users → Stripe → public launch → first 10 users. Pick the product inside the creative-automation niche so these users seed the community. A weekly Python day runs alongside the build (CS50P, one unit per week, Weeks 13–22) so Python is fluent before Phase 4 needs it.

**Phase 4 — AI Engineer Track (Weeks 25–36, Nov 30–Feb 21).** *You teach what you built.* Claude API → prompt systems → MCP → agents → art workflows → video pipelines → content engine → templates and the Creator OS → Skool foundations → founding cohort launch, Week 36. Day 252 ends with LectronicArt live.

Supplementary material that floats alongside Phase 4 rather than occupying calendar days: CS50 AI for conceptual depth when you want it, the Hugging Face Agents Course (scheduled in Weeks 28–29), and *AI Engineering* by Chip Huyen as evening reading once the API work makes it land.

## 5. The Product Ladder

People meet LectronicArt at the top of this ladder and descend at their own pace. Nothing is sold to a cold audience; every rung earns the next.

**Free content** (weekly build-in-public posts, tutorials, the meta-video) → **Free lead magnet** (the 252-Day Builder Calendar — the artifact that started everything, polished in Week 34) → **Free tools and templates** (Prompt System v1, AI Art Workflow #1 — generosity as market research) → **The Skool community** (founding cohort: low price, locked for life, capped seats; the accountability engine plus the classroom) → **Later rungs, earned not rushed:** the full template vault as a paid tier, live cohorts, eventually done-with-you builds for serious one-person businesses.

Pricing philosophy for the founding offer: price low enough that the decision is easy, scarce enough (capped seats, lifetime lock) that waiting costs something, and honest enough that "founding member" means *you're early and you'll help shape this* — because they genuinely will.

## 6. Operating Principles

These carry over from the roadmap and extend to the teaching era:

1. **Fundamentals before leverage.** For you it was six weeks of no AI code. For your members it will be a first-win onboarding before any complex automation. Nobody gets handed a machine they can't steer.
2. **Never teach what you haven't built.** Every lesson traces to a receipt.
3. **Documentation is not extra work — it's half the work.** The journal, the diagrams, the "what confused me" notes are classroom assets being produced in real time.
4. **Ship weekly, publicly.** Streaks compound; audiences attach to momentum.
5. **Plain language is the brand.** Your gift is explaining technical things to non-technical people. Every tutorial passes the test: would an artist with zero terminal experience feel respected and capable reading this?
6. **Small bets, real deadlines, honest numbers.** Every experiment has a hypothesis, a metric, and a date. Learning is the win condition, not vanity metrics.
7. **Accountability is the product.** Content libraries are everywhere. Rooms where people actually finish things are rare.
8. **Protect the streak, not the dates.** Falling behind the calendar is normal; abandoning the rhythm is the only real failure.

## 8. Risks & Anti-Patterns

**The guru trap.** Teaching outpaces building, content becomes recycled theory, and the brand joins the noise it was built to cut through. Defense: Rule One, enforced by the public archive itself.

**The shiny pipeline problem.** AI creative tools change monthly. If the curriculum is tool-specific, it rots. Defense: teach the durable layer (pipelines, prompting as engineering, the agent loop, ffmpeg, evaluation) with current tools as the rotating examples.

**Community before proof.** Launching the Skool before you have receipts means selling promises. The calendar's whole architecture — launch in Week 36, not Week 6 — exists to prevent this. Honor it even when impatience hits around September.

**Scope creep on the ten pillars.** Ten pillars is a catalog, not a to-do list for month one. The calendar sequences them; trust the sequence. The community launches with two recorded modules, not ten — and grows in public like everything else.

**Burnout.** 252 days is a long arc with a launch at each end. Sundays are load-bearing. The holiday weeks are deliberately light. Rest is written into the system because the system fails without you.
